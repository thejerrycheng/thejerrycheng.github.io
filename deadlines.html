Submission planner

The rule is simple: a paper can only be under review at one venue at a time, so the useful question is which deadline comes after the decision you are waiting for. If RSS decides today and ICRA closes next week, the same manuscript can go to RSS first and, if it is turned down, straight on to ICRA. The planner finds those windows and lines them up into a ladder.

Decisions that land before each deadline

For every upcoming deadline in the next nine months: the venues whose decision you will already have. Green means there is at least the buffer to revise; amber means the decision lands inside the buffer; red means it lands after the deadline.

Deadlines that collide

Two deadlines within ten days of each other cannot take the same manuscript. Either send two different papers or choose one.

Rules of the road

No concurrent submission. ICRA, IROS, RSS, CoRL, ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML and the CVF conferences all forbid submitting a paper that is under review elsewhere, and the ban covers substantially similar papers. Withdraw first, then resubmit.

Abstract registration is a hard gate. ICLR, NeurIPS, ICML and CVPR require an abstract a few days to two weeks before the paper. Miss it and the ladder skips that rung.

Workshops are usually exempt. Workshop papers and RA-L or T-RO journal submissions follow their own rules; check the venue's dual-submission policy for the exact wording.

arXiv is fine almost everywhere, but double-blind venues ask you not to advertise the preprint during review.

How the dates were checked

Each edition was checked against its official call for papers or important-dates page, and the supporting sentence is quoted in the edition's sources (open any row and expand Sources). Aggregators such as aideadlin.es and ccfddl were used only to cross-check, never as the source of a date; where they disagreed with the official page, the official page won and the disagreement is recorded in the notes.

Official
Every date on the row was read from an official page on the access date shown in its sources.
Est.
The edition has not published that date yet. It is projected from the last three cycles, drawn dashed, and should be treated as ±2 weeks. Turn on official dates only to hide these.
Time zones
Deadlines are shown on the calendar date the venue prints. Most close at 23:59 Anywhere-on-Earth (UTC−12); a few, such as ICRA and IROS, print Pacific time. The time and zone are shown next to each deadline when known.
Extensions
When a venue extended its deadline, the extended date is the one shown and the original is kept in the edition's detail.
Reproduce
The dataset lives in assets/data/deadlines.json, built from the research files in assets/data/deadlines_src/ by scripts/build_deadlines.py, which checks that every edition's dates are in order (abstract → paper → reviews → rebuttal → decision → camera-ready → conference) before writing the page's data.
