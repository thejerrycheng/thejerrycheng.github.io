Lunar Landing Pipeline Phases

The rocket trajectory is comprised of three key phases: (1) [cite_start]**Entry**, (2) **Flip and Altitude Capture**, and (3) **Terminal Landing Burn** to the target[cite: 131].

The Challenge

Powered descent is one of the hardest problems in spaceflight. A lander must remain stable, fuel-efficient, and robust while operating under nonlinear 6-DoF dynamics, mass depletion, actuator limits, and harsh environmental conditions.

TINTIN tackles the full difficulty of this regime: Integrated Guidance and Control (IGC) for a single-gimbaled, variable-mass lunar lander — learned end-to-end.

Fully Observable MDP

The complete rigid-body state, including pose, velocity, attitude, and mass flow.

State: \( s = [\,\mathbf{p}, \mathbf{v}, \mathbf{q}, \boldsymbol{\omega}, m\,] \in \mathbb{R}^{14} \)

6-DoF Variable-Mass Dynamics

Realistic thrust–attitude coupling and inertia scaling as propellant is consumed.

Fuel-Optimal Landing

Minimizing propellant while stabilizing attitude and controlling descent.

Model & Environment

TINTIN is trained in RocketGym — a custom MuJoCo simulator that models mass flow, gimbaled thrust, terrain geometry, and full 6-DoF physics. The system evaluates thousands of randomized trajectories to learn a globally robust descent strategy.

Lunar Dynamics

Simulated under lunar gravity (1.62 m/s²) with a maximum TWR of 3 for flip, capture, and landing burns.

State Vector

Full rigid-body state: s = [p, v, q, ω, m, I] ∈ ℝ¹⁷

Actions

Continuous control: throttle + 2-DoF gimbal u = [T, θp, θy]

Termination & Success

Termination Conditions

  • Distance > 700 m
  • Speed > 200 m/s
  • Tilt > 100°
  • Timeout (2,000 steps)

Soft Landing Criteria

  • Final radius ≤ 80 m
  • Tilt ≤ 15°
  • Touchdown velocity ≤ 20 m/s

Training Methods

TINTIN uses a Soft Actor-Critic (SAC) policy that maps full-state observations directly to engine commands. The policy is trained for stability, smoothness, and fuel efficiency.

Soft Actor-Critic

Chosen for high sample efficiency and stability. SAC consistently outperformed PPO and DQN in 6-DoF landing tasks.

Physics-Informed Rewards

Dense shaping for tilt, lateral distance, vertical velocity, and flight duration. Sparse rewards for soft landings and fuel preservation.

Curriculum Learning

Landing tolerances tighten as performance improves — enabling smooth progression from coarse to precise control.

Domain Randomization

Randomized initial pose, velocity, attitude, and mass improve global robustness across the deployment ellipse.

Key Results

RL vs Classical PID

SAC produces coordinated 6-DoF trajectories that naturally adapt to mass depletion and nonlinear coupling. The PID controller, limited by fixed gains, exhibits cross-axis instability and late-phase oscillations.

Failure Breakdown

These failures occur primarily in the final seconds of descent — suggesting opportunity for improved terminal shaping or model-based fusion.

Conclusion & Future Work

TINTIN demonstrates that deep reinforcement learning can unify guidance and control for 6-DoF lunar landing. A single SAC policy learns to coordinate thrust, attitude, and descent across thousands of randomized scenarios.

Proof of Concept

Robust global guidance and stable flip maneuvers using a single learned controller.

Terminal-Phase Challenge

Most failures occur in the final stabilization window — especially tilt regulation.

Next Steps

Integrating physics-model priors and Residual RL to enhance terminal precision and improve overall reliability.
