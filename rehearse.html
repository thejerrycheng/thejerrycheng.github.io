A real room,
as a simulator.

One 15.7-second phone video, rebuilt as a trained Gaussian room with five movable objects: a laptop with a hinge, two swivel chairs, a coffee cup and a backpack. The scene runs MuJoCo physics, with real collision geometry, right here in your browser.

Click an object (or its name) to select it, then drag the arrows to move it or switch to Rotate and drag the rings. Objects stop at the table, the floor and each other. Turn a joint with its slider or the orange handle. Drag empty space to orbit, scroll to zoom, Esc deselects.

Everything is rendered from Gaussian splats with Spark; MuJoCo 3.15 (WebAssembly) runs the same model as the exported MJCF: the same bodies, joints, masses, friction and collision shapes (boxes, cylinders and convex hulls). Where you look at a part from a side the video never saw, it is drawn with its mesh instead of a smeared splat. Polished swaps every object for its part model (seat shell, star base, lid, straps), fitted to the same video frames the way the laptop was; physics is identical in both views.     Diagnosis

    Why NeuralFeels
    falls short here.

    The NeuralFeels-style neural SDF is excellent on the laptop and cup. On the chairs and backpack it fails for measurable reasons, all of them about the capture, not the method.

    • Half of each chair was never seen.52–61 % of the chair and backpack surfaces were seen by any camera (laptop: 100 %). The chairs were only seen across about 50° of azimuth, always from above.
    • Carving turns the unseen into solid.Anything never observed as empty is kept as inside. On the right chair that is 189 L of false solid against 115 L of real chair, and 97 % of it was never seen as empty, so no silhouette can contradict it. That is why the IoU stays high.
    • The legs are below resolution.Star-base legs are about 2 cm wide, roughly 5 px from 3 m away, and only 0.6–2 % of the chair's mask pixels. None of the left chair's base is within 2 cm of its SDF.
    • Depth is worse on dark leather.Depth Anything 3 error against SfM points: 16–18 mm on the laptop and cup, 48–66 mm on the chairs.

    NeuralFeels (Suresh et al., Science Robotics 2024) assumes an object turned through every view by a robot hand, with touch filling occluded faces. One handheld pass past large dark chairs breaks both assumptions. Here it is fitted to Depth Anything 3 depth along COLMAP poses with SAM 2 masks and no touch. Diagnosis numbers ↗

    The laptop, rebuilt by an agent

    AgentSTAR (Amazon FAR, 2026) has a coding agent write a Blender script that builds the object from parts, declares its joints and iterates against the video frames. We ran its harness on 24 frames with Claude Code as the agent. Its six-part model (base, keyboard, trackpad, lid, bezel, display) and hinge are the laptop in the simulator and in the viewer above.

    Limitations

    What is measured.
    What is not.

    1. The chairs are the weakest objects. Their generated meshes reach 0.68 (right) and 0.85 (left) IoU, and the left chair's star base does not line up with the photo. Starting the neural SDF from the generated mesh is the next step.
    2. Metric scale is unverified. It comes from Depth Anything 3; the laptop's fitted width (0.43 m vs 0.356 m for a 16-inch MacBook Pro) suggests the scene is about 20 % too large. One tape measurement fixes it.
    3. Hidden surfaces are inferred. Undersides, chair backs and the floor under objects are generated or inpainted; brown patches remain on the carpet. Walls and glass have no collision.
    4. Physical properties are assumed. Masses, friction and the rigid backpack are not measured. No robot or policy has been trained in the scene yet.
