The room,
as a simulator.

One 15.7-second phone video. A trained 3D Gaussian room with the objects removed, and each object rebuilt as its own movable body: a laptop with a working hinge, two swivel chairs, a coffee cup and a backpack.

Movable objects and joints

    Click an object to select it

    Drag an object to move it; Shift + drag lifts it. Select the laptop or a chair, then drag the orange handle to open the lid or swivel the seat. Drag the background to orbit, scroll to zoom. R restores the selected pose.

    Everything you see is Gaussian splats, rendered in the browser with Spark: the cleaned room, plus each object's own Gaussians attached to its MuJoCo body frame. The chair bases were barely observed, so they are drawn with the simulator's textured meshes. “Simulation meshes” adds those meshes under every object, filling surfaces the video never saw (chair backs, the laptop's underside); they are fitted or generated, so they are less accurate than the splats. Splats farther than 1.5–2 cm from the fitted laptop and cup shapes are trimmed as floaters. Dragging here is a kinematic preview. Contact, gravity and joint friction run in MuJoCo (video below). Spherical harmonics are reduced to the base colour for download size; the decoded files match the full-precision base-colour render at 39–47 dB PSNR.

    The MJCF loads directly in MuJoCo. A small Python wrapper renders it photoreal: each link's Gaussians move with the body, the clean room is drawn in the same pass, and a robot added to the scene is composited on top with correct occlusion.

    Masses and friction are assumptions, not measurements. Physics validation passed: objects settle within 3 mm; every lifted object lands back at rest; the hinge and swivels reach their full ranges; no instabilities; 102× real time on one CPU core. The photoreal render takes 55 ms per 960 × 720 frame (112 ms with mesh hole filling) on an RTX 5080 shared with another training job.

    Move the object, keep the room

    A background
    without the objects.

    The room splat is trained from scratch with every object pixel masked out. Gaussians mostly supported inside object masks are pruned. Object footprints and contact shadows are then filled in top-down texture maps of the floor and pedestal top, so the fill looks the same from every viewpoint.

    Source frameFull-scene splatClean room splat

    Held-out frames, rendered without per-frame exposure correction. The inpainted floor patches are visible as flatter, blurrier carpet, and a faint haze remains near the glass where the right chair stood. The white charger on the pedestal side is part of the fixed room. Per-view LaMa and Stable Diffusion 2 inpainting were tried first and rejected: they smeared large holes or invented new objects.

    Limitations

    What is measured.
    What is not.

    1. Metric scale is unverified. It comes from Depth Anything 3. Cross-checks disagree by up to 15–20 %: keyboard key pitch matches, but the fitted laptop base is larger than a 16-inch MacBook Pro. One tape measurement will fix it.
    2. Hidden surfaces are inferred. The laptop's underside and lid back, the chair backs and the backpack's underside are generated or inpainted. A fully closed lid shows artifacts.
    3. Physical properties are assumed. Masses, friction and the rigid backpack are not measured. No robot or policy has been trained in the scene yet.
    4. Offline reconstruction. Every stage runs offline on one workstation (the full-scene splat alone trains in 25 minutes); nothing here runs online. Glass walls are view-dependent appearance only, with no collision.
