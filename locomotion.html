Evolution of Humanoid Locomotion Control

Sixty years of making two-legged machines stay up, in three eras: classical feedback and predictive control on physics models, reinforcement learning in massively parallel simulation, and the generative models now arriving. This page is a reading companion to the survey I co-authored — every one of its 280 references, sorted by the method it belongs to, the subject it is about and the year it appeared, each with a link to the paper itself, alongside the equations the classical methods are actually written in.

What the survey asks of the next decade

Safety and reliability first. Falling is the barrier to deployment. The paper calls for standardised recovery and impact tests, compliant and energy-dissipating hardware, and test-time adaptation, so that a humanoid outside the laboratory fails gracefully rather than catastrophically.

Then accessibility. Platform costs have already dropped by an order of magnitude; open hardware and open toolchains decide whether the field's progress is reproducible by more than a handful of groups.

Then the integration of control with perceptual and cognitive intelligence in a two-level hierarchy — a reactive motor layer under a deliberative one — and, further out, an ecosystem of networked embodied agents.

The conclusion the authors draw is that the recent leap in agility is not the triumph of neural policies alone but of contact-rich simulation, expressive architectures, physics-guided training design and asymmetric training together. Physics did not get replaced; it moved into the simulator, the reward, the curriculum and the real-time interface.

Three principles that survive every era

Physics-based modelling. Whether written down, simulated, or learned, a model of the body is what the controller reasons against.

Constrained decision making. Support polygons and torque limits in the classical era; reward shaping, curricula and action limits in the learning era; conditioning and filtering in the emerging one.

Adaptation to uncertainty. Adaptive and robust control, then domain randomisation, then in-context and test-time adaptation. The survey's argument is that these are the same problem in three vocabularies.

Getting started, as the survey recommends

Hardware: build or buy

Open-source platforms (Berkeley Humanoid, Berkeley Humanoid Lite, Stanford ToddlerBot) maximise flexibility; commercial ones (Unitree G1 and H1, Booster T1 and K1, Agility Digit, Apptronik Apollo) maximise productivity with safety-tested hardware and vendor support.

Software

Model-based: MIT Cheetah software, OpenLoong, CasADi, Acados, OCS2, Judo. Learning-based: Isaac Lab, MuJoCo Playground, MJLab, and Newton and Genesis for large-scale differentiable simulation. Data: AMASS and pipelines like BeyondMimic.

Core references

Feedback Control of Dynamic Bipedal Robot Locomotion and Robot Modeling and Control for the classical half; Reinforcement Learning: An Introduction and PPO for decision making; DreamerV3 for world models; Deep Learning, VAEs and the principles of diffusion for the generative foundations.
