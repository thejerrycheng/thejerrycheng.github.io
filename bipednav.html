BipedNav

Stairs, seen once, then walked. A monocular network turns one photo of a staircase into rise and run; a one-motor biped turns rise and run into a walking gait that is a hybrid limit cycle. Everything below was re-run from the archived code and data, and the parts that broke are reported as found.

Demonstration (1 min 20 s): the perception layer on a real staircase, the walker on flat ground, down a slope, up a 5° staircase and down a 10° staircase with treads and risers, and the geometric reason a knee-less biped cannot take steeper stairs.

Perception layer

Depth sensors on walking robots and exoskeletons are bulky; the 2024 study asked whether one RGB camera can recover the geometry that matters for a stair, its rise and run. Metric3D v2 was fine-tuned on a new dataset of 16,780 RGB-D images of indoor and outdoor stairs (Intel RealSense D415/D435, head-mounted), the metric depth was back-projected with the camera intrinsics, and planes were extracted from the point cloud.

The archived sample set has five scenes with the RGB image, the RealSense depth, the network's point cloud and normal map, and tape measurements of two steps per staircase. The re-implemented extractor works from surface normals instead of iterative RANSAC, which on these clouds fragments the grazing treads and fits planes across the nosing:

  • Normals from the organised depth grid (3-px baseline, jumps over 6 cm rejected), oriented toward the camera.
  • Predominant normals: a 4° spherical histogram; the most populated up-pointing mode is the tread normal, the walking direction is the optical axis on the tread plane, refined by a riser mode if one lies within 30° of it (side walls, whose normals are lateral, are rejected).
  • Offset clustering: tread points projected on the up axis and riser points on the forward axis form 1-D lattices whose spacings are rise and run; a least-squares lattice fit tolerates missing steps.

Deterministic, 0.09 s per scene from the RealSense cloud (0.32 s from the ASCII monocular cloud, dominated by parsing).

Locomotion layer

The walker is the acrobot biped: two identical 1 m legs (1 kg each, mass at mid-leg), point feet, no knees, no torso, one motor at the hip. The stance leg is a passive pivot on the ground; the only freedom the controller has is the angle between the legs. Walking is a hybrid system: a smooth swing phase ended by the impact of the swing foot, an instantaneous velocity jump, and a relabelling of the legs.

Virtual holonomic constraint. Instead of tracking a trajectory in time, the hip motor enforces a curve in the joint plane, q₂ = φ(θ) with θ the normalised stance angle, by input–output feedback linearisation:

τ = (H D⁻¹ B)⁻¹ ( H D⁻¹ (C q̇ + G) − Kp sin y − Kd H q̇ − Ḣ q̇ ), y = q₂ − φ(θ)

Once on the constraint, the robot behaves like a one-degree-of-freedom system with a virtual mass M(θ) and virtual potential V(θ). With δ the fraction of phase velocity that survives the impact, the step-to-step map is affine in the kinetic-like variable ζ = ½ M⁻ θ̇⁻²:

ζk+1 = (δ² / M⁻) ζk − V⁻ → stable orbit iff 0 < δ²/M⁻ < 1, V⁻ < 0, −V⁻ δ²/(M⁻ − δ²) > Vmax

The slope δ²/M⁻ is the Floquet multiplier of the gait. The design chooses a degree-5 polynomial φ from six conditions (post- and pre-impact angles, legs together at mid-step, and three tangents, one of which enforces hybrid invariance through the impact map). For slopes and stairs the coefficients and two tangent parameters are optimised under transversality, existence, stability and swing-foot clearance constraints.

Flat ground, the report's gait

25 steps at 0.302 m/s, period 1.044 s, stride 0.315 m, hip torque 110 N·m peak / 13.5 N·m rms, constraint error below 3·10⁻¹¹ rad. Started 20 % fast, the orbit converges at the analytic rate.

Down a 10° slope, exact impact map

Gait found by constrained optimisation; the swing leg retracts before touchdown (v₁ = +1.36), which keeps 68 % of the phase velocity through the impact. Floquet multiplier 0.44, 12 N·m peak torque: gravity does the work.

What the re-test found

“Honesty over polish.” Three things came out of running the archived controller against physics instead of against itself.

1

The course impact map is not the leg swap

The ECE1658 template relabels velocities after impact with a reflection, q̇⁺ = −q̇. The actual swap of stance and swing legs is q₁⁺ = q₁ + q₂ − π, q₂⁺ = 2π − q₂, whose Jacobian is [[1, 1], [0, −1]]: the new stance leg keeps the angular velocity the swing leg had. The two agree only for velocities tangent to the impact surface, which post-impact velocities are not (the old stance foot lifts off).

For the report's parameters the exact map retains δ = 0.115 of the phase velocity instead of 0.336, and the gait fails the energy test: no degree-5 design is stable on flat ground under the exact map. Retracting the swing foot before touchdown recovers δ (right), and the optimiser finds exact-map gaits with degree 7 on flat ground and a clean one on the −10° slope. Both conventions are implemented and every result on this page says which one it uses.

2

A knee-less biped cannot take human stairs

Two facts of geometry. The legs can only pass each other in the folded configuration, where the swing foot sits at the stance foot and moves perpendicular to the legs; on a flat tread that dips into the step unless the stance leg is vertical at that instant, which needs a leg aperture β > 2α for a stair of pitch α. And the swing foot moves on a circle of radius ℓ around a hip that is never higher than ℓ, so it only clears the next riser if the foot lands within b − √(2h − h²) (leg units) of the nosing.

The perceived staircase (rise 12 cm, run 28 cm, pitch 23°) would need a 46° aperture, i.e. 2.6 steps per stride for a 1 m leg, and a landing window of a few centimetres. The report's stair proof of concept (b = 1, h = 0.5, d = 0.9) used the slope model, which lets the swing foot pass 5 cm inside the tread behind the stance foot. An 80-cell sweep of fold-constrained gait designs over pitch, aperture and direction found none that is stable, clears the risers and passes transversality.

3

…but it can take shallow ones, if it places its feet right

The slope gaits were run on staircases of the same pitch and stride while sweeping the distance d from the stance foot to the next riser. Ascending a 5° staircase (rise 6.7 cm, run 76 cm) works iff the foot lands within 5 % of the tread depth from the nosing; descending 5° and 10° staircases works iff the stance foot stands on the nosing. Both are the two geometric facts above turned into a rule.

Going up: land 5 % before the edge. Going down: stand on the edge.

Up, 5° pitch, d = 0.97 b

12/12 steps, 1.02 m/s along the stair, climb rate 0.089 m/s, 77 N·m peak. The foot arrives from above with retraction, so it clears the riser it lands just behind.

Down, 10° pitch, d = 0.03 b

12/12 steps, 0.73 m/s, 9.3 N·m peak with the exact impact map (189 N·m with the course map). Leaving the fold downward, the foot is already past the tread.

Reproduce

Everything on this page is regenerated by a Python harness in BipedNav/experiments (numpy, scipy, sympy, matplotlib, OpenCV; ffmpeg for the video; no ROS, no MATLAB). The MATLAB originals remain in control/MATLAB. The technical report is the lab notebook: methods, every number, and the limitations.
